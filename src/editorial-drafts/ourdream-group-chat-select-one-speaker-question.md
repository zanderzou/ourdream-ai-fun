Select the intended speaker before sending a question. OurDream's provider-hosted community FAQ documents choosing a character portrait or typing @ and selecting a character from the list for a group-chat response.

That control helps identify the addressee. Clear wording still matters when several people and objects appear in the same scene.

## Give each role a different task

For an original adult observatory story, imagine two guides: Rowan knows the star chart, while Ellis manages the building's evening opening. You want Rowan to explain one marked star, not both characters to answer every part of a compound question.

Keeping the immediate roles distinct makes it easier to judge whether a reply fits the person you selected.

## Select, then name the narrow question

Where your current interface exposes the documented controls, choose Rowan's portrait or select Rowan from the @ list before sending.

An example message is: “Rowan, which star is marked beside the crescent symbol on this chart? Ellis can wait while you explain it.” This names the addressee and leaves the other character's next action open.

Avoid replacing that with “Can she explain it to her?” when both names have just appeared. Purdue's clear-reference guidance is useful here: make the intended person identifiable without a guessing step.

## Review who actually responded

Check the displayed speaker and the content of the reply. Does Rowan answer the chart question? Has Ellis unexpectedly supplied knowledge that belonged to Rowan? Did the response invent the player's reaction?

If the answer misses the target, correct the narrow role or question. Do not assume selection guarantees perfect dialogue behavior or that every client presents an identical group interface.

This guide does not create a group or report a performed chat test. It gives a documented selection method and an original wording example, followed by a practical check that the response came from the role you intended.
